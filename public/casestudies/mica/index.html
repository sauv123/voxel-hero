01 — Context

Housing is becoming increasingly transactional

We surveyed 15 tenants and 2 landlords in Milan to understand both sides of intergenerational housing.

Younger People

Students & Renters

  • 86.7% are students looking for affordable housing
  • 80% currently rent an apartment
  • Willing to exchange skills — cooking, tech help, companionship — for reduced rent
  • 69.2% cite freedom and noise as top concerns about co-living
Older People

Homeowners

  • Want help with companionship, grocery shopping, cooking and light housework
  • 100% value verified documentation and in-person meetings for trust
  • Top concerns: safety, privacy, noise and cultural differences
  • Most want a 1–2 month trial period before committing

There are people looking for homes and homes looking for people.

03 — What we found

Four things that changed everything

01

Housing isn't just about price

180K students in Milan's universities
72% are non-residents — looking for a place that fits their life

Traditional platforms prioritise price, location and availability. But shared living depends on lifestyle compatibility.

02

The empty room is an opportunity

320K residents aged 65+ in Milan
Many have more space than they need

The city doesn't only have a housing shortage. It has a matching problem — unused space inside people's homes isn't connected to people who need housing.

03

People will exchange value, not just money

€250–280 /month in Milan's Prendi in Casa model
650+ pairings facilitated since 2004

A room can be paid for with more than money — room for time, rent for skills, space for companionship.

04

Trust is the real barrier

650+ matches by existing Milanese programme
Few ended due to incompatibility

People don't need more listings. They need confidence in the person behind the listing.

09 — Validation

Did people understand the experience?

Usability testing with 8 participants — 4 students, 4 older adults — across two rounds of moderated sessions.

What we tested
  • Onboarding flow
  • Matching and profiles
  • Calendar interaction
  • Chat and trust features
What worked
  • Compatibility information made the match feel more personal
  • Trust signals reduced anxiety about living with a stranger
  • Exchange concept resonated with both generations
What changed
  • Calendar interaction simplified with clearer affordance
  • Added button-based alternative to swipe gestures
  • Introduced concrete exchange examples during onboarding

"The compatibility information made the match feel more personal."

— Usability testing participant

10 — Design iterations

Feedback became design decisions

Problem

Calendar interaction wasn't obvious

Users couldn't find or figure out how to select trial dates.

Insight

Users needed stronger interaction cues

The date picker looked decorative, not interactive.

Solution

Simplified interaction + clearer affordance

Tap-to-select with visual feedback and progress indicator.

Problem

Swipe gestures confused older adults

Some participants didn't know how to navigate discovery.

Insight

Not everyone thinks in swipes

Button-based actions are more universally understood.

Solution

Added tap-based alternatives

Clear Yes/No/Maybe buttons alongside the swipe gesture.

Problem

Exchange preferences felt abstract

Users didn't understand what "exchange" meant in practice.

Insight

Concrete examples beat abstract concepts

Showing real scenarios made the value exchange tangible.

Solution

Added scenario-based onboarding

Specific examples like "Cook twice a week, get 20% off rent."

Key Concern

Our validation study confirmed early concerns — 5 out of 7 users wouldn't fully trust AI-matched housing without an in-person meetup or trial period first.

This validated our trust ladder design: MICA doesn't replace human judgment — it accelerates the path from stranger to safe.

12 — Key takeaways

What I learned

Compatibility beats price

When people search for a home, lifestyle fit matters more than saving €50 on rent.

Trust is designed, not assumed

Verification, reviews and trial periods reduce anxiety more than any feature list.

Exchange is a differentiator

Turning everyday skills into housing value creates a match no traditional platform can.
