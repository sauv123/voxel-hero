Case Study Redesign Critique

A brutally honest evaluation of the olo case study — judged from the perspective of a hiring manager reviewing portfolios for Google, Stripe, Airbnb, Figma, Notion, and Microsoft.

This audit assumes the role of both a senior product designer (15+ years) and a hiring manager. The goal is not to make small improvements — it is to identify every element that weakens the portfolio's interview potential and propose a stronger alternative.

Would I Interview This Candidate?

Yes — but with reservations. The concept is compelling and the visual execution is strong enough to earn a conversation. However, the case study reads more like a project report than a strategic design narrative. Several sections lack evidence of business impact, prioritization decisions, and systems thinking — the hallmarks of senior-level work.

  • Concern: No measurable business outcomes — only vanity metrics (87% "achievable," 92% "felt safe").
  • Concern: Research depth is implied but not demonstrated — where are the raw interview transcripts, affinity diagrams, or usability test videos?
  • Concern: No trade-off analysis — what was sacrificed to ship this version? What would the next iteration look like?
  • Strength: The introvert-first framing is genuinely differentiated and well-executed.
  • Strength: Visual design is polished and consistent — the sage palette and squircle language feel intentional.

Top 10 Weaknesses

  1. No measurable business impact HIGH
    The "Impact" section shows user sentiment scores but no retention, engagement, or conversion metrics. A hiring manager needs to see how design decisions moved business KPIs.
  2. Research is surface-level HIGH
    The Discovery section shows gallery images of research artifacts but doesn't walk through the actual synthesis process — no affinity mapping, no journey pain points, no "How Might We" questions.
  3. Missing prioritization framework HIGH
    No RICE, Kano, or effort-impact matrix. The "Strategic Cuts" section is a good start but doesn't explain why certain features were deprioritized over others.
  4. No accessibility analysis MEDIUM
    The prototype mentions "optimized contrast" but there's no WCAG audit, no color-blind simulation, no screen-reader testing.
  5. Weak problem framing HIGH
    The "Problem" section states a generic issue ("high engagement, forced streaks") without anchoring it in specific user data. What % of users dropped off? What was the churn rate?
  6. No competitive analysis depth MEDIUM
    The analogy tabs compare "loud vs calm" but don't analyze specific competitors' feature sets, pricing models, or user retention strategies.
  7. Missing design system MEDIUM
    There's a "Visual Design Research" section but no documented design system — no component library, no token specification, no contribution guidelines.
  8. No usability testing protocol MEDIUM
    The Concept Validation section shows screenshots but doesn't describe the testing methodology — sample size, tasks, success criteria, or iteration cycles.
  9. Timeline is vague LOW
    "2 months" is stated but not broken down by phase. A hiring manager wants to see how work was sequenced and parallelized.
  10. No next steps or learnings MEDIUM
    The case study ends with a quote and footer. There's no "What I'd do next" section, no reflection on what worked/didn't, no acknowledgment of limitations.

Complete Redesigned Outline

The current case study has 10 sections. The redesigned version should have 12, with a different flow:

  1. 1. Hero / Context
    Project overview, team, timeline, role — condensed into a skimmable header card.
  2. 2. Problem & Opportunity
    Data-driven problem statement with specific metrics. "43% of introverted users abandoned social apps within 2 weeks due to anxiety-inducing gamification."
  3. 3. Research
    Synthesis process: interview guide, affinity map, journey map, key insights. Show the actual work, not just screenshots.
  4. 4. Personas & Scenarios
    2-3 detailed personas with behavioral maps, not just demographic cards.
  5. 5. Competitive Analysis
    Feature comparison matrix + positioning map. Show where olo fits in the landscape.
  6. 6. Opportunity Framework
    Prioritized list of opportunities with RICE scores. This is where "Strategic Cuts" should live.
  7. 7. Ideation & Concepting
    Crazy 8s, concept sketches, concept testing results. Show divergent → convergent thinking.
  8. 8. Design System
    Color, typography, components, accessibility tokens. Show the system, not just the application.
  9. 9. Prototype & Testing
    Usability test protocol, findings, iteration cycles. Show how feedback shaped the design.
  10. 10. Final Solution
    High-fidelity screens with annotations explaining key design decisions.
  11. 11. Impact & Metrics
    Pre/post metrics, user feedback, business outcomes. Include limitations and caveats.
  12. 12. Next Steps & Learnings
    What would you do next? What did you learn? What would you do differently?

Section-by-Section Analysis

Hero Section Current

Current: Large title, deck, tags, two CTAs, two phone mockups with GSAP animation.

The hero tries to do too much — it establishes the concept, shows the team, presents the timeline, and offers two CTAs. This dilutes the primary message.

The phone mockup animation is visually impressive but adds load time and doesn't communicate anything about the product.

Condense the hero to a single, clear value proposition. Move team/timeline/tags to a small metadata row. Keep one primary CTA. Replace the animated mockups with a single static hero image or a short auto-playing video.

Recommendation: Use a "context card" pattern — a small, skimmable box at the top with role, timeline, team, and tools. Let the hero focus solely on the problem and solution.
Problem Section Current

Current: Generic problem statement + analogy switcher (Traditional Apps / Introvert Reality / The olo Way).

The problem statement is generic: "Most social and habit-building applications are designed around high engagement, forced streaks, and constant reminders." No data, no specific user segment, no quantified impact.

The analogy switcher is a nice interactive element but it's decorative — it doesn't advance the narrative or provide evidence.

The "Introvert Reality" tab lists generic pain points ("Overthinking social outcomes," "Fear of judgment") without citing research.

Start with a specific, data-backed problem statement. "In user interviews, 78% of introverted participants reported feeling anxious when using gamified social apps. The primary driver was public leaderboard visibility, which 65% cited as a reason for app abandonment."

Recommendation: Replace the analogy switcher with a simple 2-column layout: problem statement on the left, supporting data/evidence on the right. Use a pull quote from a user interview to make it human.
Discovery / Research Current

Current: 4-phase grid (Competitor Analysis, Persona Dev, Behavioral Observation, Journey Mapping) with gallery images.

The gallery images are screenshots of research artifacts, but the viewer can't read them. There's no summary of what was learned.

The phase descriptions are one-liners: "Creating deep empathy for Tushar, Zaida, and others navigating anxiety." Who are Tushar and Zaida? Why are they relevant?

No mention of research methodology — how many users? What was the interview protocol? How were personas validated?

For each phase, show the actual synthesis work: affinity maps with clear themes, journey maps with annotated pain points, personas with behavioral drivers (not just demographics).

Recommendation: Use a "research synthesis" layout: for each phase, show 1) the raw artifact (blurred or summarized), 2) the key insight, 3) how it informed the design. Add a "Research Methods" callout with sample size, duration, and recruitment criteria.
Design Decisions Table Current

Current: 6-row table mapping research insights to design decisions.

This is the strongest section but it's buried in the middle. It should be more prominent.

The table is too simplistic — each row is a one-liner. Real design decisions involve trade-offs and alternatives considered.

Expand each row into a mini-case study: "Research Insight → Alternatives Considered → Decision Rationale → Outcome."

Recommendation: Move this section to immediately after research. Frame it as "Opportunity Framework" — use a 2x2 matrix (Impact vs. Effort) to show how decisions were prioritized.
Strategic Cuts Current

Current: 3-card grid (Community Feed, Public Comparison, Competitive Systems) with a "Why these cuts matter" strip.

Good concept but lacks depth. Why were these three features cut and not others? What was the prioritization framework?

No mention of what was kept and why. A hiring manager wants to see the full decision matrix, not just the cuts.

Frame this as a prioritization exercise. Show a RICE or Kano matrix with all features evaluated, not just the ones that were cut.

Recommendation: Replace the 3-card grid with a feature prioritization matrix. Include columns: Feature, Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort, RICE Score, Decision. This demonstrates systematic thinking.
Concept Validation Current

Current: 4-image gallery with a paragraph of user feedback.

The feedback is generic: "Users prioritized safety and authenticity over speed." No quotes, no data, no specific user IDs.

The gallery images are unreadable screenshots — the viewer can't extract any information from them.

Show the actual usability test protocol: tasks, success criteria, key findings with user quotes, and how feedback was incorporated into the next iteration.

Recommendation: Replace the gallery with a "Testing Report" format: Test Objective, Method, Participants, Key Findings (with quotes), Iterations Made.
Visual Design Research Current

Current: 4 insight cards (Approachability, Trust, Reduced Overwhelm, Emotional Comfort) + 2 image cards + a "Power of Squircles" callout.

The insights are generic and could apply to any wellness app. There's no introvert-specific visual research.

The "Power of Squircles" callout is interesting but feels like a design-system detail, not a research insight.

Show actual visual research: mood boards, color psychology studies, typography analysis, accessibility audits. Connect each visual decision back to a specific user need.

Recommendation: Merge this section with the Design System section. Show a "Visual Language Rationale" — for each design token (color, typography, shape), explain the user need it addresses and the research behind it.
Journey / How It Works Current

Current: 5-step interactive journey (Check In → Receive → Act → Reflect → Grow) with phone mockup switching.

This is the best interactive element but it's placed after research, which breaks the narrative flow. It should come after the solution is presented.

The step descriptions are generic and don't explain the design rationale for each step.

Move this section to after the final solution. For each step, show the screen, the user need it addresses, and the key design decision.

Recommendation: Rename to "Product Walkthrough" and place it after the solution screens. Add annotations to each phone screen explaining key interactions.
Prototype Development Current

Current: 4 evolution cards (Dense Wireframe → Streamlined Flow → Visual Hierarchy → Final Polish) with a paragraph description.

The evolution is described in prose but the cards show the actual screens. The prose doesn't match the visuals — it describes a different evolution path.

No mention of design tools, collaboration process, or stakeholder feedback loops.

Show the actual iteration process: low-fi sketches → mid-fi wireframes → hi-fi screens. For each iteration, explain what changed and why.

Recommendation: Use a vertical timeline with 3-4 key iterations. For each: thumbnail, key change, rationale, user feedback.
Product Showcase (Mockups) Current

Current: 4 full-width mockup images (Daily Life, Travel, Experience, Calm Companion).

These are beautiful but serve no purpose in a case study. They're marketing materials, not design artifacts.

No context is provided — where would these appear? What user need do they address?

Either remove these entirely or integrate them into the solution section with clear annotations explaining the design decisions shown.

Recommendation: Remove the standalone mockup section. Integrate 1-2 key mockups into the solution section with annotations.
Final Screens Gallery Current

Current: Horizontal scrollable gallery of 6 screens (Onboarding, Home, Profile, Quests, Components).

The gallery is well-implemented (arrows, dots, drag-scroll) but the screens are presented without context.

No annotations explaining key design decisions, no user flow connections, no accessibility considerations shown.

Present 2-3 key screens in detail with annotations. Show the user flow that connects them. Include accessibility notes (contrast ratios, touch targets, screen reader labels).

Recommendation: Replace the gallery with a "Key Screens" section: 2-3 screens at full width with a sidebar of annotations. Include a small flow diagram showing how they connect.
Physical Puzzle Cards Current

Current: 4-feature grid (Creates emotional attachment, Encourages offline interaction, Makes growth personal, Transforms wins into achievements) + card design specs + mockup image.

This section feels like a separate product feature, not a case study section. It's interesting but doesn't demonstrate design process.

No user research backing the physical cards concept. No business case (cost, manufacturing, distribution).

Either integrate this into the solution section as a "Physical-Digital Bridge" feature, or move it to "Next Steps" as a future opportunity.

Recommendation: Move to "Next Steps" section. Frame it as: "Based on user feedback, we identified an opportunity to extend the digital progress system into the physical world. This would require partnership with a print-on-demand service and additional user research."
Impact Section Current

Current: 3 result cards (87% Achievable, 92% Felt Safe, 64% Less Anxiety) with emoji icons.

These are vanity metrics, not business outcomes. "87% Achievable" is a survey question, not a KPI.

No baseline comparison — were these metrics better than the previous solution? What was the control group?

No sample size, no confidence intervals, no statistical significance.

Show pre/post metrics with statistical significance. Include retention rates, session duration, feature adoption. If this is a concept project, be transparent about that.

Recommendation: Replace with a "Results & Limitations" section. Show what metrics improved, acknowledge what you don't know, and be honest about the project's scope (concept vs. shipped product).
Quote / Footer Current

Current: Single user quote + footer with tagline.

The quote is generic and could be fabricated. There's no attribution beyond "Early olo user."

The footer is just a tagline — no contact info, no other projects, no way to navigate to the next case study.

If including a quote, attribute it properly (with user ID if anonymized). Replace the footer with a "Next Steps" section and a proper portfolio footer with contact links.

Recommendation: Remove the quote section. Add a "Next Steps & Learnings" section with specific, actionable insights. Include a proper footer with contact info and links to other work.

Visual Design Recommendations

Layout System

Current: 1200px max-width, 24px gutters, generous vertical spacing (120px sections). The layout is clean but some sections feel disconnected.

Adopt a consistent vertical rhythm. Use 80px section spacing for related content, 120px for major section breaks. Add subtle section dividers (1px hairline) to maintain flow without visual noise.

Recommendation: Use a 12-column grid with 64px gutters on desktop. Maintain consistent horizontal padding (24px) across all sections. Add a "back to top" button that appears after scrolling 50vh.
Typography Scale

Current: Display (56px), H1 (40px), H2 (28px), Body (16px), Small (13px). Uses Baloo Bhai 2 (rounded display) + Nunito Sans (body). The pairing works but lacks hierarchy contrast.

Add a "Display" size (64px) for hero headlines. Tighten the H1-H2 gap (40px → 32px). Use 18px for body large (lede paragraphs). Add 11px for caption/meta.

Recommendation: Use Playfair Display (serif) for editorial headlines, Inter for body. This creates a more sophisticated, senior-level aesthetic. Add negative tracking (-0.02em) on display sizes.
Color Usage

Current: Sage green (#8BA88E) as primary, terracotta (#D4956B) and periwinkle (#9BA1C1) as accents. The palette is cohesive but the accent usage is inconsistent — sometimes terracotta, sometimes periwinkle for section labels.

Use sage as the single accent. Use terracotta only for warnings/critical actions. Use periwinkle for informational elements. Remove mustard (#D4B46A) — it doesn't fit the palette.

Recommendation: Define a clear color semantics: Sage = primary action, Terracotta = warning/critical, Periwinkle = informational. Use each accent at most once per screen.
Interactive Elements

Current: Journey step switcher, analogy tabs, gallery drag-scroll, custom cursor, hamburger nav. The interactions are polished but some are unnecessary (custom cursor, analogy tabs).

Keep the journey switcher and gallery interactions. Remove the custom cursor (it's a distraction on desktop and doesn't work on mobile). Replace the analogy tabs with static content.

Recommendation: Focus interactions on content exploration, not decoration. The journey switcher is excellent — keep it. The gallery drag-scroll is great — keep it. Everything else should be simplified or removed.

Suggested Diagrams & Illustrations

Add these visual elements to strengthen the narrative:

  • Affinity Map Diagram — show how raw interview data was synthesized into themes
  • User Journey Map — visualize the emotional arc from frustration to confidence
  • Feature Prioritization Matrix — RICE or Kano model showing trade-off decisions
  • Design System Token Spec — color, typography, spacing, component library
  • Usability Test Flow — task list, success criteria, key findings, iteration cycle
  • Product Roadmap — next steps with timeline and dependencies
  • Accessibility Audit Table — WCAG criteria, compliance status, remediation plan
