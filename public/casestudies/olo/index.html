The Problem

Making Social Growth Feel High-Stakes.

Most social and habit-building applications are designed around high engagement, forced streaks, and constant reminders. For introverted and shy individuals, these systems increase fear of judgment and anxiety around initiating conversations.

📢
Loud & Performance-Driven
High pressure, constant visibility, competitive metrics

Traditional App Experience

Most platforms are designed for extroverts — reward systems based on visibility, leaderboards, and public engagement. The more you share, the more you're rewarded.

  • Public feeds and leaderboards
  • Timers and streaks creating urgency
  • Aggressive push notifications
  • Social comparison metrics

Why these cuts matter

Removing these features reduced social pressure and cognitive overload — the two primary barriers to consistent engagement for introverted users.

Validation

Concept Validation.

User feedback confirmed a preference for gradual confidence-building over instant matching, with small, pressure-free challenges proving most motivating. Users prioritized safety and authenticity over speed and wanted offline connections backed by digital reassurance. These insights validate a calm, step-by-step social support system.

Visual Design Research

🎨 Visual Design Research & Insights

The visual design research for olo focused on understanding how digital interfaces can feel emotionally safe, calm, and non-overstimulating for introverted and socially anxious users. Instead of following traditional engagement-driven UI patterns, the research explored how color, shape, spacing, typography, and interaction design influence emotional comfort and cognitive load.

We analyzed products such as Headspace, Calm, and Duolingo to understand how soft visual systems reduce anxiety and how gamification can remain motivating without becoming aggressive.

Approachability

Rounded shapes associated with safety and approachability.

Trust

Muted colors associated with calmness and deep trust.

Reduced Overwhelm

Spacious layouts with reduced cognitive load and noise.

Emotional Comfort

Minimal UI clutter focused on intentional interaction moments.

The Power of Squircles

We integrated squircles — shapes that blend characteristics of squares and circles — into challenge cards, buttons, and puzzle pieces to bridge the gap between structure and softness.

01. Emotional Awareness

Everything starts with awareness. Before any action, olo asks "How are you feeling?". We gauge your social energy level to ensure the challenges we suggest are always within your current capacity—never pushing too hard.

02. Tailored Challenges

Based on your mood, olo serves a personalized micro-challenge. Feeling quiet? The goal might be a "Passive Quest" like sitting in a park for 10 minutes. Feeling ready? It might be a "Connection Quest."

03. Real-World Action

The screen provides clear, supportive guidance during the interaction. A "Panic Button" is always available to provide a grounding exercise if the user feels overwhelmed mid-action.

04. Private Reflection

Once complete, we close the loop. Users record a quick voice reflection or select tags to process how they felt. This builds the habit of noticing small wins and reducing overthinking.

05. Visualized Progress

Growth isn't a leaderboard—it's a landscape. Every action adds a piece to your personal growth puzzle, visually expanding your comfort zone in a beautiful, non-competitive way.

Overview

Prototype Development

The Challenge Screen evolved through iterative refinements. The initial low-fidelity prototype was a dense, monochrome wireframe featuring diary and social connections. The next iteration streamlined the experience by removing non-core features, numbering steps, and adding difficulty/duration metadata. Tips were reorganized into scannable, icon-supported containers. Subsequent versions introduced color hierarchy, step illustrations, and calming, rounded typography tailored to shy or easily overwhelmed users. The final polish optimized contrast for accessibility, aligned components with the design system, and integrated subtle interactions and animations.
